Biography

Hi! I'm Yufei Wei, a Ph.D. student at the College of Control Science and Engineering, Zhejiang University (expected graduation: June 2027), advised by Prof. Yue Wang and Prof. Rong Xiong. My research focuses on 3D visual perception, world models, and embodied AI. My goal is to build robots that understand and interact with the physical world, not just move through it.

My Ph.D. work follows a line from geometric representations to world models. I started with monocular visual odometry and Bird's-Eye View (BEV) representations: the BEV-ODOM series learns metric-scale spatial representations from pose supervision alone and substantially reduces scale drift, with results published at IROS, IEEE RA-L, and IEEE T-ASE. More recently, I have turned to geometric foundations for embodied world models. G2G (CoRL 2026) builds on a frozen 3D foundation model to unify cross-sequence relocalization and multi-camera rig odometry, and StreamRig is a streaming visual odometry for multi-camera rigs built for online deployment. As a collaborator, I have also worked on vision-and-language navigation (VLN), vision-language-action (VLA) models, learning-based navigation planning, and multi-sensor fusion localization, and deployed systems on agricultural machinery, medical robots, and quadruped robots.

In industry, I have worked on two kinds of world models. At Momenta's World Model Pre-research Group, as a core team member, I worked on world-model pretraining for the R7 model, a representation-enhancing world model that predicts future visual features conditioned on the ego vehicle's future trajectory so the BEV representation learns scene dynamics; the module now runs as a core feature of R7. I am currently a World Model Algorithm Intern at the AI Lab of Astribot (星尘智能), working on the embodied world-action model Lumo-3, an action-conditioned video-generation world model for embodied manipulation, where I am responsible for action injection.

As a team captain during my undergraduate and graduate studies, I won 5 national championships and 1 national runner-up in autonomous-driving, intelligent-vehicle, and robotics competitions. In both research and deployment, I care most about building systems that actually work. If you're working on visual perception, world models, or embodied AI, feel free to reach out! 🤖

First-Author Publications

StreamRig: Exploiting Intra-Rig Geometry for Streaming Multi-Camera Odometry

Yufei Wei, Shuhao Ye, Qi Wang, Xin Zheng, Qing Huang, Rong Xiong, Yue Wang
IEEE International Conference on Robotics and Automation (ICRA), 2027 - Under Review
StreamRig treats the synchronized images of a multi-camera rig as one observation per time step. On top of a frozen 3D foundation model, it trains only a lightweight causal temporal backend with anchor snapshots, using relative-pose supervision alone, and produces multi-camera rig odometry online from continuous video streams with a state that does not grow with sequence length; both periodic re-anchoring and a long-history mode are studied. Across four platforms it has the lowest translation and rotation drift among methods that use no ground-truth aids, cuts NCLT translation error from 6.08% (pairwise chaining) to 2.77%, and, trained only on simulated data, transfers zero-shot to a real humanoid robot. With four cameras it runs at 50 Hz, faster than most monocular streaming methods.

G2G: Exploiting Intra-Group Geometry for Inter-Group Pose Estimation

Yufei Wei, Shuhao Ye, Chenxiao Hu, Yiyuan Pan, Dongyu Feng, Rong Xiong, Yue Wang, Yanmei Jiao
Conference on Robot Learning (CoRL), 2026
G2G provides a 3D-consistent geometric foundation for embodied world models through a group-to-group pose estimation framework. By freezing a 3D foundation model and adding only lightweight trainable parameters (<6% of the full model), it estimates inter-group 6-DoF poses in a single forward pass using only relative-pose supervision, unifying cross-sequence relocalization and multi-camera rig odometry while achieving state-of-the-art results across multiple indoor/outdoor datasets.

BEV-ODOM2: Enhanced BEV-based Monocular Visual Odometry with PV-BEV Fusion and Dense Flow Supervision for Ground Robots

Yufei Wei, Wangtao Lu, Sha Lu, Chenxiao Hu, Fuzhang Han, Rong Xiong, Yue Wang

BEV-ODOM2 introduces dense BEV optical flow supervision constructed directly from pose ground truth and PV-BEV dual-branch fusion to address sparse supervision and information loss without additional annotations. Achieving 40% RTE improvement over existing BEV methods, it delivers state-of-the-art performance across multiple datasets while maintaining superior scale consistency.

BEV-DWPVO: BEV-based Differentiable Weighted Procrustes for Low Scale-drift Monocular Visual Odometry on Ground

Yufei Wei, Sha Lu, Wangtao Lu, Rong Xiong, Yue Wang
IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters (RA-L), 2025
BEV-DWPVO leverages unified metric-scaled BEV representation and differentiable weighted Procrustes solver to address scale drift in monocular visual odometry. By simplifying pose estimation to 3-DoF and training end-to-end with only pose supervision, it achieves best average RTE of 8.67% and 6.92% on NCLT and Oxford datasets, delivering superior scale consistency without requiring depth estimation or segmentation supervision.

BEV-ODOM: Reducing Scale Drift in Monocular Visual Odometry with BEV Representation

Yufei Wei, Sha Lu, Fuzhang Han, Rong Xiong, Yue Wang
2024 IEEE/RSJ International Conference on Intelligent Robots and Systems (IROS), pp. 349-356
BEV-ODOM is the first to demonstrate that BEV representation alone, without segmentation or auxiliary tasks, can achieve low scale drift in monocular visual odometry. Using correlation-based feature extraction and 3-DoF pose regression with only pose supervision, it reveals that scale consistency originates from BEV's inherent metric-scaled grid structure rather than side-tasks, providing a simpler and more interpretable approach.

Co-authored Publications

MATCH: Execution-Aware Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning for Vision-and-Language Navigation in Continuous Environment

Shuhao Ye, Yufei Wei, Demao Wang, Shichao Zhai, Rong Xiong, Yue Wang
IEEE International Conference on Robotics and Automation (ICRA), 2027 - Under Review
MATCH targets vision-and-language navigation in continuous environments (VLN-CE) by jointly training a language-conditioned planner and a goal-conditioned primitive-action executor with hierarchical RL, both learning from shared on-policy navigation rollouts. An execution-aware topological graph stores the executor's value estimates and realized outcomes for each frontier target, so the planner can choose execution requests and refine targets within a bounded neighborhood; a transition-aware dense macro reward trades ordered progress along the reference route against execution residual, so local success does not replace instruction following. With 0.5B parameters, MATCH reaches state-of-the-art results on R2R-CE and RxR-CE, and has completed a preliminary real-robot deployment.

Field experiments demonstrate errors below 4.38cm and over 90% trajectory points within 7.5cm threshold using vision-only localization, validating its feasibility for practical agricultural automation.

Robotwin-HI: A Scenario-Oriented Reverse-Design Benchmark for VLA Robustness

Xin Zheng, Anzhe Chen, Yufei Wei, Qing Huang, Rong Xiong, Yue Wang
IEEE International Conference on Robotics and Automation (ICRA), 2027 - Under Review
Robotwin-HI is a VLA robustness benchmark built on a reverse-design principle: instead of enumerating perturbation dimensions and combining them, it starts from household and industrial deployment scenarios and derives scenario-specific perturbation ranges and co-occurrence patterns. The benchmark has 20 main tasks and a separate five-task object-generalization split, organizes deployment changes into Vision, Embodiment, and Semantics, and uses Clean, Robust-NoBase, and Robust-Base conditions to separate nominal task capability, scenario-level deployment robustness, and base-pose robustness. Experiments on representative VLA policies show that strong Clean performance can drop substantially under deployment changes, base-pose variation causes further failures, and cross-category object transfer remains hard.

Learning to Tune a Mobile Robot Planner: Hierarchical Architecture and Sim-to-Real Transfer

The Cyclic Co-Training strategy enables stable learning of coupled policies, while the Terrain-Adaptive Controller achieves robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer by maintaining consistent tracking performance across diverse terrains, outperforming state-of-the-art auto-tuning and end-to-end methods.

Revisiting Topological Graphs for Macro Action based Closed-loop Reinforcement Learning of Vision Language Navigation in Continuous Environment

Shuhao Ye, Sitong Mao, Yuxiang Cui, Yufei Wei, Xuan Yu, Shichao Zhai, Wen Chen, Shunbo Zhou, Rong Xiong, Yue Wang
2026 IEEE/RSJ International Conference on Intelligent Robots and Systems (IROS)
This work reformulates vision-and-language navigation in continuous environments (VLN-CE) as a hierarchical MDP, abstracting the scene into a topological graph so that a high-level policy plans over a macro-action space of frontier nodes while a training-free low-level controller serves as the state transition, drastically shortening the decision horizon to make closed-loop reinforcement learning feasible. An action-aware value head evaluates state values under the dynamic frontier action space to drive a graph-based PPO, achieving state-of-the-art performance on the R2R-CE and RxR-CE benchmarks.

PathTime-VLA: Path-Time Decoupling for Factorized Post-Training of Vision-Language-Action Policies

Qing Huang, Yifei Yang, Ziqing Zou, Anzhe Chen, Zhenjie Zhu, Yufei Wei, Rong Xiong, Yue Wang
IEEE International Conference on Robotics and Automation (ICRA), 2027 - Under Review
PathTime-VLA brings the path-time parameterization of classical motion planning into the action space of a VLA: the policy predicts a progress-indexed interaction path and a separate interval-time profile, so execution speed can change without changing the geometric target. This split supports staged post-training, with demonstrations and DAgger shaping the path prior, Speed-DQN learning execution speed from real-robot interaction, and Path-AWR refining the path generator from rollout outcomes. Across three real-robot tasks it reaches 58/60 successes versus 57/60 for BC + DAgger, with about 39–52% shorter mean completion time.

TDC: Sim-to-Real Transferable Directional Compliance for Contact-Rich Manipulation

Yifei Yang, Anzhe Chen, Zhenjie Zhu, Kechun Xu, Yunxuan Mao, Yufei Wei, Lu Chen, Rong Xiong, Yue Wang
Conference on Robot Learning (CoRL), 2026
TDC (Transferable Directional Compliance) achieves adaptive compliance in contact-rich manipulation using only simulation data. Its key insight is that force regulation decomposes into a time-varying but sim-to-real transferable directional component, governed by task geometry, and a dynamics-sensitive but manually tunable magnitude component. The directional component is instantiated as a task frame and per-axis control gains predicted by a visuomotor policy adapted from a pre-trained VLA model and trained by imitation learning on automatically generated simulation demonstrations; at deployment, an admittance controller fuses these predictions with human-specified stiffness and target wrench to realize adaptive compliance. Across four real-world tasks (microwave opening, peg-in-hole insertion, whiteboard wiping, and door opening), it attains strong success rates and robustness to external disturbances.

🏫 Volunteer Teaching at Gucheng Primary School, Yulin, Guangxi

Over three unforgettable weeks of humid, unpredictable weather, I went from fumbling through my first lessons to confidently running a classroom, learning responsibility, patience, and what it truly means to be there for someone, through lesson planning, class management, home visits, and countless cycles of breaking down and starting over again. After leaving Gucheng Primary School, I still often think of the children doing homework under the dusk light and bidding me farewell in the moonlight on that last night. May my clumsy yet sincere efforts back then have left even a little warm and lasting light in their lives.

💼 Entrepreneurship

During my undergraduate years, I founded and led an 11-person founding team to launch a smart ergonomic chair project, dedicated to addressing the back and lower-back health problems caused by prolonged sitting. Our goal was to deliver a personalized support experience beyond conventional seating through a backrest system that can sense, learn, and actively support. I led the overall product planning and technical roadmap, driving the solution and the team's coordination across the full process, from market research and user-needs analysis to feature definition, system architecture design, and implementation.

Our core technology combined a pressure-sensing matrix, back sensors, and machine-learning algorithms to accurately identify the user's identity, hip position, and sitting posture, and to adjust support strength automatically. The support system used a combination of mechanical actuators and multi-zone air bladders: the mechanical part handles large-range adjustment while the bladders provide fine tuning, supporting fully automatic, semi-automatic, and manual modes. Through an app and physical buttons, the system continuously learned and optimized for each user's habits, achieving personalized back-following and lumbar-sacral support. In this process, I led the team to analyze problems from first principles and to validate the solution through minimal-subsystem iteration, successfully completing three generations of functional prototypes that covered the full pipeline from structural design and control circuitry to algorithm validation, supply chain, and patent strategy, and ultimately raised angel-round funding.
