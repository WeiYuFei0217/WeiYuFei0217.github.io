Biography

Hi! I'm Yufei Wei, a Ph.D. student at the College of Control Science and Engineering, Zhejiang University, advised by Prof. Yue Wang and Prof. Rong Xiong. My research centers on 3D visual perception, world models, and embodied AI — I'm driven by the vision of enabling robots to genuinely understand and interact with the real world, not just move through it.

My earlier work focused on monocular visual odometry, Bird's-Eye View representations, and visual localization, published in journals and conferences including IEEE RA-L and IROS. These efforts addressed real-world challenges in reducing scale drift and achieving robust localization for mobile robots in complex environments. As a collaborator, I've also contributed to projects spanning learning-based navigation planning, multi-sensor fusion localization, and applications in agricultural automation, medical robotics, and quadruped robots. Currently, I'm exploring world model construction and vision-and-language navigation, focusing on cross-modal integration through unified representations, and leveraging multimodal LLMs for scene understanding and navigation tasks.

During my undergraduate years, I led teams to five national championships in autonomous driving and robotics competitions, contributed to the PaddlePaddle open-source community, and developed a lightweight autonomous delivery vehicle. I also served as team leader for an intelligent ergonomic chair startup at Zexiang Li's Entrepreneurship Incubator in Shenzhen and spent time teaching at a primary school in Yulin, Guangxi. During my internship at Shenzhen InnoX Academy, I contributed to developing a semantic line segment mapping-based LiDAR-IMU SLAM system and obstacle avoidance algorithms for autonomous street sweeping vehicles. More recently, as a core team member at Momenta's World Model Pre-research Group, I worked on a next-generation end-to-end autonomous-driving world model. Whether in research or real-world deployment, I prioritize building systems that actually work over chasing theoretical elegance alone.

I'm always excited to collaborate, discuss ideas, and learn from others. If you're working on visual perception, world models, embodied AI, or anything that brings robots closer to understanding our world, let's chat! 🤖

G2G: Exploiting Intra-Group Geometry for Inter-Group Pose Estimation

Yufei Wei*, Shuhao Ye*, Chenxiao Hu, Yiyuan Pan, Dongyu Feng, Rong Xiong, Yue Wang, Yanmei Jiao† (* equal contribution, † corresponding author)
arXiv preprint - Under Review
G2G provides a 3D-consistent geometric foundation for embodied world models through a group-to-group pose estimation framework. By freezing a multi-view foundation model and adding only lightweight trainable parameters (<6% of the full model), it estimates inter-group 6-DoF poses in a single forward pass using only relative-pose supervision, unifying cross-sequence relocalization and multi-camera rig odometry while achieving state-of-the-art results across multiple indoor/outdoor datasets. BEV-DWPVO: BEV-based Differentiable Weighted Procrustes for Low Scale-drift Monocular Visual Odometry on Ground

Yufei Wei, Sha Lu, Wangtao Lu, Rong Xiong, Yue Wang
IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters (RA-L), 2025
BEV-DWPVO leverages unified metric-scaled BEV representation and differentiable weighted Procrustes solver to address scale drift in monocular visual odometry. By simplifying pose estimation to 3-DoF and training end-to-end with only pose supervision, it achieves best average RTE of 8.67% and 6.92% on NCLT and Oxford datasets, delivering superior scale consistency without requiring depth estimation or segmentation supervision.

BEV-ODOM: Reducing Scale Drift in Monocular Visual Odometry with BEV Representation

Yufei Wei, Sha Lu, Fuzhang Han, Rong Xiong, Yue Wang
2024 IEEE/RSJ International Conference on Intelligent Robots and Systems (IROS), pp. 349-356
BEV-ODOM is the first to demonstrate that BEV representation alone, without segmentation or auxiliary tasks, can achieve low scale drift in monocular visual odometry. Using correlation-based feature extraction and 3-DoF pose regression with only pose supervision, it reveals that scale consistency originates from BEV's inherent metric-scaled grid structure rather than side-tasks, providing a simpler and more interpretable approach.

Yufei Wei, Fuzhang Han, Yanmei Jiao, Zhuqing Zhang, Yiyuan Pan, Wenjun Huang, Li Tang, Huan Yin, Xiaqing Ding, Rong Xiong, Yue Wang

TDC: Sim-to-Real Transferable Directional Compliance for Contact-Rich Manipulation

Yifei Yang, Anzhe Chen, Zhenjie Zhu, Kechun Xu, Yunxuan Mao, Yufei Wei, Lu Chen, Rong Xiong, Yue Wang
arXiv preprint - Under Review
TDC (Transferable Directional Compliance) achieves adaptive compliance in contact-rich manipulation using only simulation data. Its key insight is that force regulation decomposes into a time-varying but sim-to-real transferable directional component, governed by task geometry, and a dynamics-sensitive but manually tunable magnitude component. The directional component is instantiated as a task frame and per-axis control gains predicted by a visuomotor policy adapted from a pre-trained VLA model and trained by imitation learning on automatically generated simulation demonstrations; at deployment, an admittance controller fuses these predictions with human-specified stiffness and target wrench to realize adaptive compliance. Across four real-world tasks (microwave opening, peg-in-hole insertion, whiteboard wiping, and door opening), it attains strong success rates and robustness to external disturbances. (Under Review)
Paper

Revisiting Topological Graphs for Macro Action based Closed-loop Reinforcement Learning of Vision Language Navigation in Continuous Environment

Shuhao Ye, Sitong Mao, Yuxiang Cui, Yufei Wei, Xuan Yu, Shichao Zhai, Wen Chen, Shunbo Zhou, Rong Xiong, Yue Wang
2026 IEEE/RSJ International Conference on Intelligent Robots and Systems (IROS) - Accepted
This work reformulates vision-and-language navigation in continuous environments (VLN-CE) as a hierarchical MDP, abstracting the scene into a topological graph so that a high-level policy plans over a macro-action space of frontier nodes while a training-free low-level controller serves as the state transition, drastically shortening the decision horizon to make closed-loop reinforcement learning feasible. An action-aware value head evaluates state values under the dynamic frontier action space to drive a graph-based PPO, achieving state-of-the-art performance on the R2R-CE and RxR-CE benchmarks.

Beyond Research

🏫 Volunteer Teaching at Gucheng Primary School, Yulin, Guangxi

In the summer of 2019, I joined a volunteer teaching team at Gucheng Primary School in Yulin, Guangxi, serving as both an English teacher and a homeroom teacher. Over three unforgettable weeks of humid, unpredictable weather, I went from fumbling through my first lessons to confidently running a classroom, learning responsibility, patience, and what it truly means to be there for someone, through lesson planning, class management, home visits, and countless cycles of breaking down and starting over again. A student's "Teacher, your class is so much fun," a few homework assignments that were finally no longer copied, and the sight of the children running freely across the playground after a rainstorm were always the source of strength that carried me through fever, conflict, and self-doubt.

As a child, I once watched a documentary about human trafficking, and ever since I have carried the wish to help solve it. That dream still feels distant, but in this remote mountain village school, in that humid summer of 2019, I understood for the first time that education and protection are the first line of defense against harm, and a skylight opened for these children under the deep mountain sky. After leaving Gucheng Primary School, I still often think of the children doing homework under the dusk light and bidding me farewell in the moonlight on that last night. May my clumsy yet sincere efforts back then have left even a little warm and lasting light in their lives.

💼 Entrepreneurship

During my undergraduate years, I founded and led an 11-person founding team to launch a smart ergonomic chair project, dedicated to addressing the back and lower-back health problems caused by prolonged sitting. Our goal was to deliver a personalized support experience beyond conventional seating through a backrest system that can sense, learn, and actively support. I led the overall product planning and technical roadmap, driving the solution and the team's coordination across the full process, from market research and user-needs analysis to feature definition, system architecture design, and implementation.

Our core technology combined a pressure-sensing matrix, back sensors, and machine-learning algorithms to accurately identify the user's identity, hip position, and sitting posture, and to adjust support strength automatically. The support system used a combination of mechanical actuators and multi-zone air bladders: the mechanical part handles large-range adjustment while the bladders provide fine tuning, supporting fully automatic, semi-automatic, and manual modes. Through an app and physical buttons, the system continuously learned and optimized for each user's habits, achieving personalized back-following and lumbar-sacral support. In this process, I led the team to analyze problems from first principles and to validate the solution through minimal-subsystem iteration, successfully completing three generations of functional prototypes that covered the full pipeline from structural design and control circuitry to algorithm validation, supply chain, and patent strategy, and ultimately securing an angel-round funding opportunity.
